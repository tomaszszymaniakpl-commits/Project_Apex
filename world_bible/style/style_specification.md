# Style Specification - Zootopia Thriller Project

*Stworzony na podstawie analizy Take A Stand & Out of the Woods*

---

## NARRACJA
- **POV:** Third person limited, dual POV (Nick + Judy)
- **Tense:** Past tense 
- **POV switching:** Between chapters or clear breaks (***) only
- **Information asymmetry:** Controlled release for mystery, dramatic irony for emotional impact
- **Language:** English (target: older teens/adults, mature themes acceptable)

---

## SKŁADNIA & RYTM

### Sentence Structure
- **Average length:** 20-21 words per sentence
- **Distribution:** 
  - Short (1-10 words): 15-18% - use for impact, emotional beats
  - Medium (11-20 words): 40-45% - standard narrative flow  
  - Long (21+ words): 40-45% - complex scenes, atmosphere building

### Complexity Guidelines
- **Simple:complex ratio:** ~35:65 (favor complex for sophistication)
- **Punctuation:** 
  - Semicolons (;) frequently for compound thoughts
  - Dashes (—) rarely - prefer semicolons
  - Fragments acceptable in dialogue ("My bad.", "Deal.")
  - Rhetorical questions in internal monologue

### Dynamic Pacing
- **Action scenes:** Shorter sentences, fragments OK, present tense verbs for momentum
- **Emotional scenes:** Longer, complex sentences with introspection
- **Dialogue scenes:** Mix of short ripostes and occasional monologs

---

## SŁOWNICTWO

### Language Levels
- **Casual:** 35-45% (dialogue, internal thoughts)
- **Neutral:** 45-50% (standard narrative)  
- **Literary:** 10-15% (atmospheric moments, key scenes)

### ZOOTOPIA-SPECIFIC TERMS
**Must Use:**
- **"Inters"** - inter-species couples (not "interspecies relationships")
- **"gentlemammal"** - gentleman equivalent
- **Brand names:** "Snarlbucks" (coffee), "Furbook" (social media)

### ANIMAL ANATOMY (Critical)
- **Ratio:** 85% animal terms, 15% human terms
- **Always use:** "paws" not "hands", "muzzle" not "mouth/face"  
- **Consistent:** "ears", "tail", "fur" throughout
- **Only use "hands":** For specific medical/technical contexts

### ENHANCED SENSORY LANGUAGE
- **Priority:** Scent > Sound > Visual > Touch
- **Scent examples:** "fox musk", "predator scent", "fear-smell"
- **Sound examples:** Enhanced hearing details, species-specific vocalizations
- **Visual:** Species-appropriate (night vision for nocturnal, etc.)

---

## DIALOGI

### Format Distribution  
- **Said tags:** 60-65%
- **Action tags:** 25-30%
- **Bare dialogue:** 10-15%

### Character Voice Patterns

#### **NICK WILDE:**
- **Cynicism/sarcasm:** 35-45% of lines have edge
- **Nicknames:** "Carrots" (primary), "Fluff", "Spots","Snowaball" He likes to give nicknames to everyone
- **Deflection tactics:** Avoids direct answers, uses humor
- **Catchphrases:** "You can't kid a kidder", "For shame, Carrots"
- **Length:** Prefers short ripostes to long explanations
- **Register shift:** Street casual ↔ professional as needed

#### **JUDY HOPPS:**
- **Police jargon:** 30% in professional contexts ("ten-four", "Bravo-Six", "dispatch")
- **Idealism markers:** "justice", "right thing", "make the world better"
- **Determination:** Uses full names in serious moments, commands
- **Catchphrases:** "sweet cheese and crackers", "dumb fox" (affectionate)
- **Empathy:** "motherly tone" descriptions, emotional support language

### Species-Based Speech
- **Predators:** Growl/rumble undertones in stress
- **Prey:** Higher/faster speech when nervous, squeaks  
- **Size correlation:** Large = slow/commanding, small = fast/emotional

---

## OPISY LOKACJI

### Length Guidelines
- **Standard scenes:** 1 paragraph max
- **Key locations:** 2-3 paragraphs for first introduction
- **Functional:** Brief scene-setting, integrated into action

### Sensory Priority (Animal-Enhanced)
1. **Scent (25%):** Species-specific detection, emotional responses
2. **Visual (50%):** POV-appropriate (fox night vision, rabbit wide angle)  
3. **Sound (15%):** Enhanced hearing, species recognition
4. **Touch (10%):** Fur, temperature, texture through paws

### POV Species Differences
- **Nick (fox):** Enhanced night vision, predator scent analysis, superstitions
- **Judy (rabbit):** Prey alertness, escape route awareness, sound detection
- **Others:** Appropriate to their biology (wolf pack sense, etc.)

---

## SOCIAL DYNAMICS INTEGRATION

### Inter-Species Relationships
- **Default assumption:** Politically divisive, personally challenging
- **Microaggressions:** Subtle concern trolling, species purity comments
- **Professional bias:** Merit overcomes species in good organizations (ZPD)
- **Family pressure:** Generational differences, gradual acceptance

### Stereotype Navigation
- **Characters fight expectations:** Active resistance to species roles
- **Internal conflict:** Self-doubt about living up to/defying stereotypes  
- **Social proof:** Need to demonstrate worth beyond species assumptions

---

## TECHNIKI NARRACYJNE

### Information Management
- **Flashbacks:** Clear time markers or smooth italics transition
- **Internal monologue:** Italics for direct thoughts, single quotes occasionally
- **Foreshadowing:** Through animal instincts, biological responses
- **Mystery elements:** Clues through enhanced senses, species advantages

### Scene Transitions  
- **Prefer:** Dramatic cuts between scenes
- **Occasional:** Smooth flow for emotional continuity
- **Chapter breaks:** Natural POV switches, tension peaks

---

## DO-NOT LIST (AI-isms to Avoid)

### Generic Phrases (Replace With Animal Equivalents)
- ❌ "Heart pounding like a drum" → ✅ "pulse prey-quick", "heart hammering"
- ❌ "Time stood still" → ✅ Species-appropriate equivalent
- ❌ "Hands trembling" → ✅ "Paws shaking"
- ❌ "His face turned red" → ✅ "Flush visible through fur"

### Biological Errors
- ❌ Ignoring enhanced senses (fox not smelling, rabbit not hearing)
- ❌ Species abilities ignored for plot convenience  
- ❌ Human-only metaphors without biological grounding
- ❌ Over-the-top animal puns in serious moments

### Character Voice Violations
- ❌ Nick giving long earnest speeches without irony
- ❌ Judy abandoning police professionalism without cause
- ❌ Generic dialogue that could be anyone

---

## MATURE CONTENT GUIDELINES

### Acceptable Elements (Target: Older Teens/Adults)
- **Language:** Profanity in appropriate contexts (stress, anger, criminals)
- **Violence:** Police procedural level (gun violence, injury, death)
- **Romance:** Adult relationship development, physical intimacy implied
- **Themes:** Corruption, betrayal, discrimination, moral complexity

### Content Integration
- **Profanity:** Character-appropriate (street vs. professional contexts)
- **Violence:** Consequences-focused, not gratuitous
- **Romance:** Emotional development priority over physical
- **Discrimination:** Realistic portrayal, educational value
