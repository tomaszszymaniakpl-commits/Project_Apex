# Chapter 16: The Setup

The morning sun had barely crested the horizon, casting long, pale shadows across the Canal District. 

Judy sat in the passenger seat of an unmarked sedan, a pair of high-powered binoculars pressed to her eyes. Two blocks away, the SyncPoint Research Facility sat like a concrete fortress at the edge of the water. It was a massive, windowless block of brutalist architecture, surrounded by a high-security perimeter fence, active camera sweeps, and private security patrols. 

"Primary approach has to be the south access road," Wolford murmured from the driver's seat, tracing a line on a physical map resting on the steering wheel. "It's the only route that can handle the turning radius of a heavy flatbed."

"Agreed," Judy said, lowering the binoculars. "McHorn, how does the north face look?"

From the back seat, the rhino shifted his bulk, peering through a telephoto lens. "Solid concrete. No loading bays. Just a single personnel door with a biometric lock. If they try to exfil that way, they'll be bottlenecked."

Judy nodded, her mind already laying out the tactical grid. "Leni, do you have eyes on the roof of that textile mill across the street?"

*“I'm looking at the satellite topography now,”* Leni’s voice crackled softly over the encrypted radio. *“It's a flat roof, four stories up. Perfect line of sight to the SyncPoint loading bay. I can set up a sniper hide behind the old HVAC units. They'll never see me.”*

"Good," Judy said, a cold, hard satisfaction settling in her chest. 

For the last three days, they had been reacting to traps, stumbling into decoys, and bleeding on the asphalt. But not today. Today, they had the map. They had the target. They were finally a step ahead of the white fox. 

"I've tapped into the municipal traffic cameras on the three main arterials leading to the facility," Leni continued. *“If a heavy transport convoy rolls into this sector, my system will flag it instantly.”*

"Then there's no reason for us to sit here and risk getting spotted by a ZIA patrol," Judy decided, rolling her window up. "We have the perimeter locked. Let's head back to the precinct and wait for the bell to ring."

***

By ten in the morning, the bullpen at Precinct One was a hive of mundane activity. Telephones rang, keyboards clattered, and the smell of cheap coffee permeated the air. 

Judy sat at her desk, staring at a half-finished incident report on her monitor. She typed a sentence, deleted it, and typed it again. 

The waiting was excruciating. 

They didn't know if the hit was going to happen today, tomorrow, or next week. Every time a phone rang, Judy’s ears twitched. Every time a shadow crossed the room, her muscles tensed. 

She glanced to her left. Wolford was sitting at his desk, perfectly still. To anyone else, he looked like a bored cop staring into space. But Judy could see his paws resting inside the half-open drawer of his desk, methodically field-stripping, cleaning, and reassembling his service weapon, over and over again. 

Across the room, McHorn was aggressively organizing a stack of files, his massive hands completely steady, his eyes constantly flicking toward the digital dispatch board. 

They were a coiled spring, hiding in plain sight. 

"Hey, guys!" 

Judy jumped slightly as Clawhauser suddenly appeared beside her desk. The cheetah was holding a pink pastry box, and for the first time in two days, he was actually smiling. 

"Ben," Judy breathed, forcing her heart rate back down. "What is it?"

"Two things," Clawhauser said, his voice dropping to an excited whisper. He looked around to make sure no one was listening. "First... I just got off the phone with Zootopia General. It's Nadine. She's awake."

Wolford slammed his desk drawer shut. He stood up, his yellow eyes wide. "What?"

"The doctors don't understand it," Clawhauser beamed, practically vibrating with relief. "They said her vitals stabilized overnight. The paralysis is completely gone. She's weak, and they're keeping her for observation, but... she's going to be okay. She's going to make it."

A collective, silent breath left the Strike Force. Wolford closed his eyes, leaning heavily on his desk. McHorn let out a low, rumbling sigh of pure relief. 

Judy looked at the empty chair at Fangmeyer’s desk. 

The relief washed over her, but it didn't extinguish the cold fire burning in her chest. It only fed it. Nadine was going to live, but that didn't change what had happened. It didn't change the fact that Nick had stood by while a sniper put a dart in her friend's neck. 

"What's the second thing, Ben?" Judy asked, her voice steadying. 

Clawhauser’s smile faded, replaced by a look of bewildered shock. He leaned in even closer. "A federal bulletin just came across the primary wire. It's Jack Savage."

Judy’s ears snapped forward. "What about him?"

"He's burned," Clawhauser whispered. "ZIA just issued a shoot-on-sight warrant for him. They're saying he's a mole. That he's been working for the syndicate this whole time."

Wolford let out a harsh, bitter laugh. "Well. That explains a lot."

"It explains everything," Judy said, her eyes narrowing as the final pieces of the puzzle clicked into place. "That's how they were always three steps ahead. That's how they knew our patrol routes, how they knew we were at the decoy warehouse, how they knew where Nadine lived. Savage was feeding them our playbooks."

"He stood right in front of us at the Annex and let them walk out the back door," McHorn growled, his fists clenching. 

"Not today," Judy said, looking back at her monitor, the words blurring together. 

She was the Commander now. Whenever Leni’s alarm went off, whether it was in five minutes or five days, Judy would be the one to give the order to breach. She would be the one to pull the trigger. If she hesitated, if she let her past with Nick cloud her judgment for even a fraction of a second, her team would die. 

*I won't hesitate,* Judy promised herself, her violet eyes hardening into chips of dark ice. *I'm going to burn them down.*

***

Deep underground, the air in the bunker was heavy and stale, smelling of gun oil and nervous sweat. 

Nick sat on the edge of his cot in the small, windowless room he shared with Skye. A single, harsh bulb illuminated the metal tray resting on his knees. His paws moved with practiced, mechanical precision, assembling a small, cylindrical device out of scavenged medical tubing, a heavy-duty spring, and a pressurized canister. 

It looked like a crude, oversized auto-injector. 

The heavy steel door clicked and swung open. Nick didn't jump. He just smoothly laid a towel over the tray as Skye stepped inside and pushed the door shut behind her, engaging the deadbolt. 

She looked at the towel, then up at his face. "Do you have it?"

"Yeah," Nick said quietly. He pulled the towel away, picked up the finished device, and slid it into the flat pouch stitched along the inside of his tactical vest—rib-high, nothing that would print through fabric. He pressed the flap until it seated. "Our life insurance policy is fully underwritten."

Skye let out a slow breath, sitting down on the cot next to him. She was already wearing her white tactical gear, her fur blending seamlessly into the Kevlar weave. 

"Ragnar wants us in the armory in five minutes," Skye said, staring at the blank concrete wall opposite them. "The transport convoy is fueled and ready."

"Then this is it," Nick murmured. 

"This is it," Skye agreed. She finally turned to look at him, her pale blue eyes stripping away the layers of sarcasm and bravado they both wore like armor. "Nick. We need to be clear about today."

"I know the plan, Snowball."

"I'm not talking about the plan. I'm talking about the margins." Skye leaned closer, her voice dropping to a harsh, urgent whisper. "If we get inside the SyncPoint facility and something goes wrong... if Vesper catches me slicing the network, or if Ragnar decides you're a liability..."

"We fight our way out," Nick said instantly. 

"No." Skye grabbed his wrist, her claws digging slightly into his fur. "No, we don't. If one of us gets made, the other one doesn't stop. You don't try to save me, and I don't try to save you. Whoever is still breathing finishes the job. You cut the wire, Nick. Even if they have a gun to my head."

Nick stared at her. The cold logic of it was undeniable, but hearing it spoken aloud felt like swallowing broken glass. He had spent his entire life protecting her. 

"Skye—"

"Promise me," she demanded, her grip tightening. "Zero margin, Nick. We don't walk away from this until the Architect is blind and deaf. Promise me."

Nick looked into his sister's eyes. He saw the same terrifying resolve that had kept them alive on the streets of the district when they were cubs. 

"Zero margin," Nick whispered. "I promise."

Skye let go of his wrist and stood up, her posture instantly shifting back into the cold, untouchable mercenary. "Let's go gear up."

They walked out of the quarters and down the corridor to the main armory. 

The room was a hive of aggressive, tightly wound energy. Two dozen of Ragnar’s mercenaries were strapping on heavy ceramic plates and checking the action on military-grade assault rifles. In the corner, Vesper Nyx was carefully loading a row of clear, glass-tipped darts into a specialized bandolier, her golden eyes completely focused on the lethal chemistry. 

Ragnar stood at the head of the room, a massive, custom-built light machine gun slung across his chest. He looked like a warlord preparing for a siege. 

Nick walked over to a metal table lined with ammunition crates. He picked up his standard-issue sidearm, checked the slide, and reached for a box of magazines. 

Skye leaned against the edge of the table next to him, watching his paws as he methodically thumbed heavy, blunt-nosed rounds into the spring-loaded clip. 

She offered a small, ironic smile. "Don't hurt yourself with that, brother."

Nick didn't look up. He slammed the final round into the magazine, slapped it into the grip of the pistol, and racked the slide with a sharp, metallic *clack*. 

He looked at Skye, a slow, easy grin spreading across his muzzle. 

"Don't worry about me, Snowball," Nick said, holstering the weapon at his hip. "I always play it safe."

***

The mobile command center was housed inside a reinforced, unmarked heavy transport truck parked in the lower levels of the bunker. The interior was bathed in the cool, blue glow of a dozen high-end tactical monitors. 

Vesper sat at the primary console, her fingers dancing across the keyboard with terrifying speed. Ragnar stood behind her, his massive arms crossed, staring at the digital map of Zootopia. Nick and Skye stood near the rear doors, fully geared up, waiting for the green light. 

"The decoy teams are in position at the Sahara Square power grid and the Rainforest District water mains," Vesper reported, her tufted ears twitching as she monitored the encrypted comms. "They're waiting for your word, Ragnar."

"Tell them to hold," Ragnar rumbled, his deep voice vibrating in the confined space. He looked at Nick. "You're absolutely sure the ghost-loops on those sub-stations will hold when the pressure spikes?"

"They'll hold," Nick said smoothly, leaning against the metal wall. "The bypasses are hardcoded into the municipal diagnostic cycle. The city's system will think the pressure drops are just routine maintenance until it's too late to stop the cascade."

Ragnar grunted, a sound that was almost a compliment. "We'll see, fox."

Vesper tapped a final key. "The network is primed. We have a secure audio link to the Architect."

The main screen shifted from the city map to a simple, pulsating audio waveform. 

"I am here," a voice spoke through the truck's speakers. It was deep, cultured, and perfectly calm. It didn't sound like a terrorist. It sounded like a CEO addressing a board meeting. 

"The board is set, sir," Vesper said, her tone shifting to one of absolute deference. "Decoy teams are holding. The primary strike team is ready to move on the SyncPoint facility."

"Excellent," the Architect said. His voice carried a chilling, philosophical weight. "For decades, this city has operated under the illusion of stability. A fragile ecosystem built on outdated infrastructure and political compromises. Today, we remove the illusion."

Nick kept his face perfectly still, but he could feel Skye tense beside him. 

"When the lights go out and the water stops flowing, the citizens will realize how vulnerable they truly are," the Architect continued. "And when the panic sets in, they won't look to their politicians or their police for salvation. They will look to whoever can turn the lights back on. They will look to the apex of the new order."

The waveform pulsed steadily. 

"It is time to evolve," the Architect said softly. "Initiate the Apex protocol."

Vesper didn't hesitate. She hit the *Enter* key. 

The screens inside the truck instantly flashed from cool blue to violent crimson. 

"Glitch is live," Vesper announced, her fingers flying as she monitored the data streams. "Decoy teams are executing the physical breaches. Ghost-loops are engaging."

For the first few minutes, nothing seemed to happen. The map of Zootopia remained relatively calm. Nick watched the clock in the corner of the screen tick upward. Five minutes. Ten minutes. The tension in the truck was thick enough to cut with a knife. 

Then, at exactly twelve minutes post-breach, the cascade began. 

It didn't happen all at once. It started as a ripple. A single red warning icon blossomed in the Canal District. Then three more in Sahara Square. 

"Canal District reporting a massive pressure drop in the primary water mains," Vesper read off the screen, a dark smile spreading across her muzzle. "Sahara Square grid is experiencing rolling blackouts. The traffic control algorithms in the central hub are trying to reroute power, but the ghost-loops are feeding them false telemetry."

The ripple turned into a wave. Red icons flooded the map, multiplying exponentially as the city's automated systems fought against themselves, creating a feedback loop of catastrophic failures. 

"The municipal dispatch servers are already flooding with automated panic alerts," Vesper said, her voice rising with dark excitement. "The ZPD is going to be completely blind in five minutes."

Ragnar let out a low, terrifying laugh. He watched the map of the city tear itself apart, his scarred face twisted into a brutal grin. 

He turned to Nick. 

"I have to admit, fox," Ragnar said, the hostility finally giving way to genuine, predatory respect. "You did good work with those sub-stations. The city is bleeding out exactly on schedule."

Nick forced a smirk, tapping his temple. "You can't kid a kidder, Ragnar."

"Enjoy the view while it lasts," Ragnar said, grabbing his heavy machine gun from the rack. The celebration was over in an instant. He kicked the rear doors of the truck open, letting the stale air of the bunker flood in. "The city is distracted. The police are chasing ghosts. Let's go get the Core."

Nick and Skye followed the reptile out of the truck, stepping into the staging area where a single, unmarked tactical SUV waited for them. No mercenaries. No backup. Just the four of them. 

The Apex. 

***

The lights in the bullpen of Precinct One flickered once, buzzed loudly, and died. 

For two seconds, the room was plunged into absolute darkness. Then, with a heavy, mechanical thud, the backup generators kicked in, bathing the precinct in the harsh, red glow of emergency lighting. 

Before anyone could ask what had happened, the phones started ringing. 

Not one or two. Every single landline on every single desk in the bullpen began to scream simultaneously. The massive digital dispatch board at the front of the room rebooted, instantly filling with a cascading waterfall of red alert icons. 

"Multiple traffic collisions in Sahara Square!" Francine yelled over the din, pressing a headset to her ear. "The stoplights are completely down!"

"I've got a water main burst in the Rainforest District!" another officer shouted. "It's flooding the lower transit tunnels!"

Judy stood up slowly, the red light reflecting in her eyes. The noise in the room was deafening. The panic was immediate and visceral. 

She looked toward the glass doors of the server room. Leni was standing there, her face pale, holding up a single finger. 

*It's starting.*

The door to the Chief's office flew open. Bogo stepped out onto the landing, his massive silhouette framed by the red emergency lights. He grabbed a bullhorn from the wall bracket. 

"Listen up!" Bogo's voice boomed over the ringing phones, instantly silencing the room. "We have a city-wide infrastructure failure. All leave is canceled. All ongoing investigations are suspended. I want every available officer on the street right now! Priority is traffic control and civilian evacuation in the flooded sectors! Move!"

The bullpen erupted into motion. Officers scrambled for their gear, grabbing radios and keys, rushing toward the garage to help a city that was suddenly tearing itself apart. 

Judy watched them go. Her heart hammered against her ribs. Every instinct she had as a police officer screamed at her to grab her vest, get in a cruiser, and go help the civilians trapped in the flooding tunnels. It was what she had sworn an oath to do. 

But if she did that, the Architect won. 

If she spent the next four hours directing traffic in Sahara Square, the syndicate would walk out of the Canal District with the Master Synchronization Core, and the city would never truly belong to its citizens again. 

She had to let the city bleed today to save it tomorrow. 

Judy turned her back on the scrambling officers. She looked at Wolford and McHorn. They were watching her, waiting for the order. 

"Armory," Judy said, her voice barely audible over the chaos, but hard as steel. "Heavy gear."

They moved against the tide of police officers, slipping into the precinct's tactical armory. They didn't bother checking out the equipment. McHorn grabbed two heavy ballistic shields and a breaching shotgun. Wolford loaded up on flashbangs and extra magazines for his assault rifle. Judy strapped on a heavy Kevlar vest over her uniform and grabbed a compact submachine gun. 

When they walked back out into the corridor, fully geared for war, a few patrol officers stopped and stared at them in shock. Nobody said a word. The look in Judy’s eyes dared anyone to try and stop her. 

They headed for the rear garage. 

As they passed the front desk, Judy saw Clawhauser. The cheetah was frantically typing on his console, his fur standing on end, completely overwhelmed by the sheer volume of emergency calls. 

Judy stopped. She needed a safety net. She needed someone on the outside. 

"Ben," Judy said, leaning over the desk. 

Clawhauser jumped, looking at her heavy tactical gear with wide eyes. "Judy? What are you doing? The Chief said—"

"Listen to me very carefully," Judy interrupted, her voice a fierce, urgent whisper. "We are going off-book. We are going to stop the people causing this."

"But... but where are you going?"

"SyncPoint Research Facility. Canal District," Judy said, making sure he heard every syllable. "You do not tell anyone that location. Not Bogo, not dispatch. You keep it to yourself."

Clawhauser swallowed hard, his paws trembling. "Okay. Okay, I won't tell anyone."

"If you don't hear from me on the encrypted channel in exactly one hour... or if my radio goes dead before then... that's when you call in the cavalry," Judy instructed, her eyes burning into his. "If we go dark, you send the entire SWAT division to that address. But not a second before. Do you understand?"

"SyncPoint. Canal District. One hour," Clawhauser repeated, nodding frantically. "If you go dark, I send SWAT."

"Good." Judy squeezed his paw once. "We're going to fix this."

She turned and ran toward the garage, where Wolford had already started the engine of an unmarked, heavily armored tactical van. Judy climbed into the passenger seat, slamming the heavy door shut. 

"Leni is in the back with McHorn," Wolford said, gripping the steering wheel. "Where to, Commander?"

"Canal District," Judy said, racking the bolt of her weapon. "Drive."

The van tore out of the precinct garage, plunging into the chaotic, red-lit streets of a city falling apart.
