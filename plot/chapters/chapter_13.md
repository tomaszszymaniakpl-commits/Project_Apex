# Chapter 13: Near Miss

The afternoon sun beat down on the corrugated metal roof of Leni’s garage, turning the interior into a slow cooker. 

Strike Force stood around the central workbench, fully geared up. They weren't wearing the unmarked black Kevlar from the morning before. Today, they wore standard ZPD tactical armor, complete with badges, precinct patches, and body cameras. 

If they were going to kick down a door in broad daylight, they had to look like they belonged there. 

"Listen up," Fangmeyer said, her voice cutting through the hum of the cooling fans. She tapped the map of the Cliffside Power Annex on the board. "We are walking into a confined space with unknown hostiles who are heavily armed and highly organized. No hero runs. We move as a unit. We secure the hardware, we secure any suspects, and we get out."

"And if we see the white fox?" Wolford asked, checking the action on his service weapon. 

"We take her alive," Judy said instantly. Her voice was hard, but her violet eyes darkened with something deeply personal. "She's the only one who knows exactly what Nick is doing. And I want to hear her explain it to me."

McHorn grunted, adjusting the heavy ballistic plates over his chest. "If she shoots first, Hopps, I'm not asking her to surrender twice."

Judy met the rhino's eyes. "Understood. But try."

"What about me?" Leni asked, zipping up a lightweight tactical jacket over her usual t-shirt. She was practically vibrating with nervous energy, holding a sleek black helmet under one arm. "I've got the mobile decryption rig packed. And my SWAT cert is just as current as yours. Are you finally letting me out of the cave?"

"You're on overwatch, Leni," Judy said, tossing her a set of encrypted comms. "You take the bike, set up on the ridge above the Annex, and monitor their local network. If they try to trigger another ghost-loop or wipe their servers while we're breaching, you lock them out."

Leni caught the earpiece, her ears drooping slightly. "So I'm just tech support again. I can breach a door, Judy. I didn't pass the tactical course just to sit on a hill."

"We know you can," Fangmeyer said, her voice softening slightly. "But if their systems go dark while we're inside, we're blind. We need our best weapon on the network, Leni. That's you."

Leni let out a sigh, though a small, proud smile tugged at the corner of her mouth. "Fine. I'll ruin their digital day. But next time, I'm kicking down the door."

Fangmeyer looked around the room. "Are we clear on the threshold?"

"If we take heavy fire, or if ZIA shows up, we pull back," Wolford recited the rule they had agreed upon. "We don't get into a turf war with federal agents, and we don't die for a pile of stolen relays."

"Good." Fangmeyer picked up her radio. "Time to make it official."

She keyed the mic, switching to the primary dispatch channel. "Dispatch, this is Lieutenant Fangmeyer, Car 14. We are following up on a lead regarding the commercial burglary at Sub-Station 12. Requesting a case file attachment for a high-risk trespass at the Cliffside Power Annex, Sector Four."

There was a brief pause, filled with static. 

*“Copy that, Car 14,”* the dispatcher replied. *“Logging your location. Be advised, Sector Four is a designated industrial zone. Initiating deconfliction protocol to ensure no overlapping operations.”*

Fangmeyer lowered the radio. "We're on the board. Let's move."

They piled into two unmarked, but officially registered, ZPD cruisers—with Leni trailing behind on her low-profile pursuit motorcycle—and pulled out of the alley, merging into the heavy afternoon traffic heading toward the Cliffside district. 

Ten minutes into the drive, Fangmeyer's radio crackled to life. It wasn't dispatch. 

*“Car 14, this is SWAT Actual. You copy, Fangmeyer?”*

Fangmeyer frowned, picking up the mic. "I copy, Delgato. What can I do for you?"

*“Just saw your deconfliction flag pop up on the board,”* the lion’s deep, smooth voice came through the speaker. *“You guys are hitting the Cliffside Annex? That’s a big piece of concrete for a standard detective unit to clear.”*

"We have Hopps, Wolford, and McHorn with us," Fangmeyer replied, keeping her tone professional. "We all have our tactical certs. We're just checking a lead on some stolen hardware. Nothing we can't handle."

*“I don't doubt it,”* Delgato said, his tone perfectly collegial, almost brotherly. *“But with the Black Warrant out on Wilde and his crew, the Chief has us on high alert. I want to make sure my people aren't going to trip over yours if things go sideways. I'm setting up a rapid response perimeter just in case you need us.”*

Fangmeyer exchanged a look with Judy in the passenger seat. It made sense. It was standard procedure for SWAT to monitor high-risk calls. 

"Appreciate the backup, Delgato," Fangmeyer said. 

*“Just looking out for my own,”* Delgato replied smoothly. *“Give me your exact entry vector and your ETA so I can position my snipers to cover your blind spots.”*

Fangmeyer didn't hesitate. "We're approaching from the south. We'll be breaching through Gate C, Ramp 04. ETA is exactly twelve minutes."

*“Gate C, Ramp 04. Twelve minutes,”* Delgato repeated, his voice calm and steady. *“Copy that, Hopps. We'll keep the channel clear. Happy hunting.”*

The radio clicked off. 

"Nice to know somebody at the precinct still has our backs," Wolford muttered, keeping his eyes on the road. 

"Yeah," Judy said, looking out the window as the massive, concrete cooling towers of the Cliffside Annex loomed into view. "Let's just hope we don't need them."

***

The Cliffside Power Annex was a brutalist concrete monolith, surrounded by a high, barbed-wire fence and acres of cracked asphalt. 

Strike Force approached in two vehicles, killing their sirens and lights three blocks away. They rolled to a stop just outside the visual range of Gate C. 

"Overwatch is in position," Leni’s voice crackled over the encrypted comms. "I'm looking at the Annex network right now. The ghost-loop is still active. They're definitely inside, and they think they're invisible."

"Copy that, Leni," Fangmeyer said, stepping out of the lead cruiser. She racked the bolt of her tactical rifle. "Keep the channel clear unless they try to wipe the servers."

Judy stepped out of the passenger side, her paws checking the spare magazines on her tactical vest. The heat coming off the asphalt was intense, but she barely felt it. Her heart was hammering a steady, predatory rhythm against her ribs. 

"Wolford, you take the left flank," Fangmeyer ordered, her voice dropping to a low, commanding register. "McHorn, you're on the heavy ram. Hopps and I will stack behind you. We breach Ramp 04, fan out, and secure the primary control room."

"Left flank clear," Wolford confirmed, drawing his sidearm and moving toward the chain-link fence. 

"Ram is ready," McHorn grunted, lifting the heavy steel battering tool as if it weighed nothing. 

"We move on my mark," Fangmeyer said, raising two fingers. "In three. Two..."

The screech of heavy tires tore through the afternoon air. 

Three matte-black SUVs came tearing around the corner of the access road, moving at reckless speed. They didn't slow down. They swerved violently, cutting off the ZPD cruisers and slamming to a halt directly in front of Gate C, blocking the Strike Force's path to the ramp. 

"What the hell?" Wolford barked, raising his weapon. 

The doors of the SUVs flew open simultaneously. A dozen mammals in heavy, unmarked tactical gear poured out, assault rifles raised and leveled directly at the ZPD officers. 

"ZIA! Stand down and drop your weapons!" a voice boomed through a PA system. 

Judy didn't drop her gun. She kept it aimed squarely at the chest of a massive polar bear stepping out of the lead vehicle. "ZPD, this is our scene! Lower your weapons!"

"This is a federal scene now, Officer Hopps."

Jack Savage stepped out from behind the polar bear. He wasn't wearing a suit today. He wore a sleek, black tactical vest over a dark shirt, his striped ears pinned back flat against his skull. His eyes were cold, hard, and utterly devoid of the polite professionalism he usually wore. 

"Agent Savage," Fangmeyer snarled, stepping forward, her rifle still raised. "You are interfering with an active police operation."

"I am securing a federal crime scene," Jack countered, his voice slicing through the tension like a scalpel. He didn't even look at Fangmeyer. His eyes were locked on Judy. "This location is now under the direct authority of the Zootopia Intelligence Agency. Lower your weapons, or my agents will consider you hostile combatants."

"You don't have a warrant for this address," Judy yelled back, her grip on her sidearm tightening. "We tracked them here! They're inside right now!"

"And they are classified under a Black Warrant," Jack said, taking a slow, deliberate step forward. "Which means they are out of your league, Hopps. You don't have the clearance, the training, or the authority to be here. Put the gun down before you get your team killed."

"You're giving them time to run!" Judy shouted, the realization hitting her like a physical blow. Jack wasn't just pulling rank. He was stalling. He was standing in the open, arguing with local cops, while the most dangerous terrorists in the city were fifty yards away. 

"I am giving you a direct federal order," Jack said, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "Stand down."

McHorn shifted his weight, the heavy ram still in his hands. "Lieutenant?" he asked Fangmeyer, his voice a low rumble. 

Fangmeyer looked at the dozen ZIA agents, their laser sights painting red dots across the ZPD armor. She gritted her teeth, the muscles in her jaw bulging. 

"Lower your weapons," Fangmeyer ordered bitterly. 

"Nadine!" Judy protested. 

"I said lower them, Hopps! That's an order."

Slowly, agonizingly, Wolford and McHorn lowered their gear. Judy kept her gun raised for three more seconds, her violet eyes burning into Jack's. The rabbit agent stared back, completely unyielding. 

With a sharp exhale of pure frustration, Judy lowered her weapon. 

Jack gave a curt nod to his men. "Secure the perimeter. Team Alpha, prepare to breach the main—"

The deafening roar of a high-performance engine drowned out his words. 

From the secondary loading ramp, fifty yards to their right, a sleek, white motorcycle tore out of the shadows. The rider was clad in dark tactical gear, leaning low over the handlebars, wearing a black helmet with a mirrored visor. 

But beneath the edge of the jacket, a long, bushy tail streamed in the wind. 

A fox. 

Judy’s heart slammed against her ribs. *Nick.* 

The rider didn't immediately flee. They gunned the engine, the rear tire spinning and kicking up a cloud of acrid white smoke, holding the bike in place for one agonizing second. Making sure everyone saw them. Making sure they were the only target that mattered. 

Then the front wheel slammed down, and the motorcycle launched forward, tearing down the access road toward the canal. 

"Target is on the move!" Jack roared, spinning away from Judy. "Alpha team, with me! Bravo, secure the building!"

Two of the ZIA SUVs threw themselves into reverse, tires shrieking in protest, before tearing off after the motorcycle. 

Judy took a step toward her cruiser, pure instinct taking over. "We have to go—"

"No!" Fangmeyer grabbed Judy’s shoulder, her grip like a steel vise. "Hopps, stop! ZIA has faster cars, and we don't have pursuit jurisdiction! You pull out into that traffic, Savage will have you arrested for reckless endangerment before you clear the district!"

"That's Nick!" Judy yelled, struggling against the tiger's grip. "Nadine, let me go!"

"I can't!"

The screech of another set of tires cut through the chaos. 

Leni skidded into the staging area on her low-profile pursuit motorcycle, the engine whining as she slammed on the brakes. She kicked the kickstand down, ripped her helmet off, and looked at the ZIA agents swarming the main doors. 

"Guys!" Leni yelled over the noise. "The network just flatlined! Someone wiped the servers from the inside! The ghost-loop is gone!"

Judy stopped struggling. She looked at the cloud of tire smoke fading down the access road. She looked at the ZIA agents breaching the Annex. And then she looked at Leni’s motorcycle. 

The engine was still running. 

Judy didn't think. She just moved. 

She ducked under Fangmeyer’s arm, sprinted the ten yards to the bike, and ripped the helmet out of Leni’s paws. 

"Hey!" Leni yelped, stumbling back as Judy swung her leg over the seat. "What are you doing?!"

"Borrowing this!" Judy shouted, jamming the helmet onto her head. 

"Hopps, don't you dare!" Fangmeyer roared, lunging forward. 

Judy dumped the clutch. The heavy pursuit bike surged forward with terrifying torque, leaving a black streak of rubber on the asphalt. She leaned hard into the turn, slipping right past the remaining ZIA SUV, and shot down the access road after the white smoke. 

Leni stood in the middle of the staging area, her paws empty, staring at the empty road. "Did... did she just steal my bike?"

"Son of a—" Fangmeyer cursed, spinning around toward the cruiser. "McHorn! Wolford! Leni! In the car! Now!"

Wolford didn't move toward the cruiser. He was staring at the open doors of Ramp 04. The Bravo team of ZIA agents had just disappeared inside, their flashlights cutting through the gloom. 

"Lieutenant," Wolford said, his voice low and urgent. "Go. Get her before she kills herself."

Fangmeyer stopped at the driver's side door. "What about you?"

"I'm going to see what the fox left behind," Wolford said, drawing his sidearm and stepping into the shadows of the concrete wall. "Go!"

Fangmeyer didn't argue. She threw the cruiser into gear and tore off after Judy, leaving Wolford alone in the heat. 

The wolf took a slow breath, letting his eyes adjust to the darkness inside the Annex. The air pouring out of the open doors smelled like melting plastic and ozone. 

He slipped inside, moving silently along the perimeter wall. 

The main floor was a chaotic mess of heavy crates and thick cabling, but the ZIA agents were entirely focused on clearing the catwalks and the primary control room. Wolford kept his head down, using the massive cooling pumps as cover. 

He found the makeshift command center tucked behind a row of transformers. 

It was a disaster. Two server racks were actively smoking, their hard drives melted into slag by thermite charges. A folding table had been flipped over, papers scattered everywhere. 

Wolford holstered his weapon and dropped to one knee. He had maybe sixty seconds before ZIA swept this sector. 

He sifted through the debris. Most of the papers were blank or burned beyond recognition. But under the edge of the flipped table, shielded from the thermite blast, he found a half-burned fragment of a schematic map. 

It had a single alphanumeric code stamped in the corner: *SYN-RND-C4*. 

Wolford carefully folded the fragment and shoved it into his vest. 

He stood up, his eyes scanning the area. Near the smoking servers sat a heavy, reinforced transport crate. It was empty, the interior lined with custom-molded foam. Whatever it had held, it was the only thing the fox had cared enough to take with them. 

Wolford drew his combat knife, stepped up to the crate, and quickly sliced the shipping manifest sticker off the side. It had a long, complex serial number printed on it. 

"Bravo Two, sweep the lower quadrant!" a voice echoed from the other side of the transformers. Heavy boots crunched on the concrete, moving closer. 

Wolford slipped the sticker into his pocket, melted back into the shadows, and ghosted his way toward the side exit, leaving the ZIA to guard an empty, burned-out shell. 

***

The wind screamed past Judy’s helmet as she tore through the industrial district. Leni’s pursuit bike was a terrifying machine, stripped of all weight and tuned for raw acceleration. Judy wove through the afternoon traffic, her heart hammering against her ribs, her eyes locked on the faint, dissipating trail of white tire smoke ahead. 

*Why, Nick?* 

The question beat against the inside of her skull, matching the rhythm of the engine. She had spent three months mourning him. She had spent the last two days hunting a ghost. But seeing him on that screen in the bullpen, standing next to a monster like Ragnar Drakov... it had cracked something inside her. It wasn't just betrayal anymore. It was an obsession. She needed to tackle him off that bike. She needed to pin him to the concrete and scream the question into his face until he gave her an answer. She needed to know if any of it had been real, or if she had just been another mark in his longest con. 

*“Hopps! What the hell are you doing?!”* Fangmeyer’s voice exploded in Judy’s earpiece, loud enough to make her wince. 

"I'm not letting him go, Nadine!" Judy shouted back, leaning hard into a sharp right turn that scraped the bike's footpeg against the asphalt. 

*“You don't even know where he went! You're flying blind!”*

"Then give me eyes!" Judy snapped. "Leni, are you on the network?"

There was a second of static, followed by the rapid clatter of a keyboard. *“I'm in the city traffic grid,”* Leni’s voice came through, tight and focused. *“I don't have the bike on camera yet, but I'm tracking the ZIA convoy. They just blew through the intersection at Miller and 5th, heading west.”*

"They're pushing him toward the canal," Judy realized, gunning the throttle. "Give me an intercept route."

*“Take the next left down the service alley. It'll bypass the main arterial.”* Leni paused, her voice pitching up. *“Wait. Got him. Traffic cam on 9th Street. The bike just cut across three lanes and went down the embankment. He's heading for the old storm drain tunnels under the canal.”*

"Copy that," Judy said. "I'm two minutes out."

*“Judy, listen to me,”* Fangmeyer cut back in, her voice losing its anger, replaced by a heavy, terrifying seriousness. *“ZIA SUVs won't fit down those tunnels. But you will. If you go in there alone, you are completely off the grid. We can't cover you.”*

"I know," Judy said softly. "I have to do this, Nadine."

She cut the comms. 

Judy hit the embankment at sixty miles an hour, the bike's suspension absorbing the brutal drop as she transitioned from asphalt to the slick, moss-covered concrete of the canal basin. Ahead of her, the massive, gaping maw of the primary storm drain loomed like a black void. 

She plunged into the darkness. 

The roar of the engine echoed deafeningly off the curved concrete walls. She flicked on the high beams. A hundred yards ahead, the white motorcycle was lying on its side, the front wheel still spinning lazily. 

Abandoned. 

Judy slammed on the brakes, throwing the bike into a controlled slide. She didn't even bother with the kickstand. She let the bike drop, drawing her sidearm as she hit the ground rolling. 

She came up in a crouch, sweeping the darkness. The tunnel smelled of stagnant water, rust, and the sharp tang of hot exhaust. 

A shadow moved near a rusted maintenance ladder bolted to the wall. 

"Nick!" Judy yelled, her voice echoing down the endless pipe. "Stop!"

The figure froze. They were dressed in dark tactical gear, their back to her, one foot already on the first rung of the ladder. 

Judy closed the distance, her gun raised, her paws shaking slightly. "Turn around. Slowly. Keep your paws where I can see them."

The figure didn't raise their paws. Instead, they turned, moving with a fluid, terrifying grace. 

Judy stepped forward to grab the collar of the tactical jacket, intending to slam the suspect against the wall. It was a standard ZPD takedown. 

It didn't work. 

The figure didn't resist the grab; they flowed with it. A hand clamped over Judy’s wrist, twisting it sharply outward. Before Judy could process the pain, a boot swept her legs out from under her. 

Judy hit the concrete hard, the breath exploding from her lungs. Her gun clattered away into the darkness. 

She scrambled to recover, throwing a desperate punch, but the figure deflected it effortlessly, stepping inside her guard and driving a knee into her stomach. Judy gasped, doubling over. The figure grabbed her tactical vest, spun her around, and slammed her back against the rusted rungs of the ladder. 

The impact rattled Judy’s teeth. A forearm pressed against her throat, pinning her in place. 

Judy looked up, her vision swimming, ready to scream Nick's name. 

But the face looking down at her wasn't a red fox. 

During the scuffle, the mirrored helmet had been knocked loose. It hit the concrete and rolled away. 

Pale blue eyes stared back at her, cold and empty as a frozen lake. White fur framed a sharp, aristocratic muzzle. 

It wasn't Nick. 

"You," Judy gasped, the air burning in her throat. The white vixen from the bridge. The ghost. 

Skye didn't blink. She kept her forearm pressed against Judy’s windpipe, applying just enough pressure to control her, but not enough to crush. 

"Where is he?" Judy demanded, her voice a ragged hiss, struggling against the vixen's grip. "Where's Nick?!"

"You're out of your depth, Officer Hopps," Skye said. Her voice was smooth, cultured, and utterly devoid of emotion. "You're chasing shadows in a game you don't understand."

"I understand that you took him!" Judy snarled, kicking out. 

Skye shifted her weight, easily absorbing the blow, and pressed harder against Judy’s throat. The world edged in black. 

"Nobody took him," Skye whispered, leaning in close. She smelled of ozone and expensive gun oil. "He made his choice. Now you need to make yours."

Judy tried to reach for the knife on her belt, but Skye caught the movement. With a blur of motion, the vixen swept Judy’s arm aside, grabbed her by the shoulders, and threw her to the ground with bone-jarring force. 

Judy hit the wet concrete, gasping for air, her muscles screaming in protest. She tried to push herself up. 

A heavy tactical boot planted itself firmly in the center of her chest, pinning her to the floor. 

Judy looked up. Skye stood over her, silhouetted against the glare of the fallen motorcycle's headlight. The vixen wasn't even breathing hard. The fight hadn't been a fight at all; it had been a dismissal. 

"Don't get up," Skye said, her voice dropping to a chilling, absolute zero. "Stop looking for us. Stop digging into the logistics. Walk away."

Judy glared up at her, defiance burning through the pain. "Or what?"

Skye leaned down, her pale eyes locking onto Judy’s violet ones. 

"Or next time, I won't be so gentle," Skye said softly. "If you take one more step into this... someone you love is going to die."

Skye stepped off Judy’s chest. She didn't run. She simply turned, climbed the rusted maintenance ladder with silent, effortless speed, and disappeared into the darkness of the upper access shaft. 

Judy lay on the wet concrete, the sound of the idling motorcycle echoing in the empty tunnel. She was bruised, exhausted, and completely defeated. 

She had finally caught the ghost. And the ghost had let her live. 

***

Ten minutes later, the wail of police sirens echoed down the concrete access shaft. 

Flashlight beams cut through the gloom of the storm drain, sweeping over the stagnant water and the abandoned white motorcycle. 

"Over here!" McHorn’s voice boomed, bouncing off the curved walls. 

Fangmeyer was the first to reach her. Judy was sitting on the wet concrete, her back pressed against the rusted rungs of the maintenance ladder. She had her knees pulled up to her chest, one paw resting lightly over her bruised windpipe. She wasn't crying. She was just staring blankly at the dark water of the canal. 

"Hopps." Fangmeyer dropped to one knee, her flashlight illuminating the scrapes on Judy’s face and the dirt ground into her tactical vest. "Are you hit? Where are you hurt?"

Judy didn't look at her. She didn't look at Leni, who was standing a few feet away, looking at her stolen motorcycle with a mixture of relief and anger. 

"She didn't even breathe hard," Judy whispered, her voice a raspy, painful croak. 

"What?" Fangmeyer asked, leaning closer. "Judy, look at me. Did she shoot you?"

"She could have," Judy said, her violet eyes finally shifting to meet the tiger's gaze. The absolute defeat in them was harder to look at than any bullet wound. "She had a knife. She had me pinned. She could have snapped my neck in two seconds. But she didn't."

McHorn stepped up behind Fangmeyer, his heavy brow furrowed. "Then why are you still breathing?"

"Because killing me would have made me a martyr," Judy said, the bitter truth tasting like ash in her mouth. "She didn't want to kill me. She wanted to humiliate me. She wanted to show me exactly how outclassed we are."

The sound of heavy boots splashing through the water announced Wolford's arrival. The wolf jogged out of the darkness, his sidearm holstered, looking between the abandoned bike and Judy sitting on the floor. 

"ZIA is locking down the Annex," Wolford reported, his chest heaving slightly. "Savage is furious. He's got agents sweeping the whole grid."

"Did you get anything before they locked it down?" Fangmeyer asked, standing up. 

Wolford reached into his vest and pulled out the half-burned map fragment and the shipping sticker. "Just this. A partial schematic and a serial number off an empty crate. I don't even know what it means yet."

Judy looked at the scraps of paper in Wolford's paw. Two days ago, she would have seized them as a victory. Now, they just looked like garbage. 

"It means they got exactly what they came for," Judy said, slowly pushing herself up from the wall. Her legs trembled slightly, but she refused to let McHorn help her. "And they left us the scraps."

Fangmeyer looked at the bruised rabbit, then at the exhausted faces of her team. They had broken protocol, stolen a vehicle, clashed with federal agents, and lost their primary target. 

"Wrap it up," Fangmeyer said, her voice heavy with finality. "McHorn, get the bike. Wolford, secure those scraps. We're taking Hopps to the precinct medic."

"Nadine, I'm fine—" Judy started to protest, her voice cracking. 

"You're not fine, Judy," Fangmeyer cut her off, her tone leaving absolutely no room for argument. "We got played. We got beat. We are done for the day."

Judy looked back down the dark, empty tunnel where the white fox had vanished. The ghost had won. 

"Yeah," Judy whispered, touching her bruised throat. "We're done."

***

The heavy steel door of the subterranean bunker slammed shut with a force that shook the concrete walls. 

Ragnar Drakov stormed into the main hall, his massive reptilian chest heaving. He didn't stop moving until he reached the central planning table. With a furious roar, he swept his thick arm across the surface, sending encrypted laptops, tactical radios, and coffee mugs smashing against the far wall. 

"Ragnar," Vesper purred from the doorway, stepping inside and brushing a speck of dust from her pristine coat. "Do try to control your temper. Hardware is expensive."

"We ran like rats!" Ragnar bellowed, spinning to face her. His scarred snout was pulled back, exposing rows of razor-sharp teeth. "We had to burn our own servers and crawl through a mile of raw sewage because of one rabbit!"

Nick and Skye walked into the room a moment later. Nick was carrying two heavy duffel bags filled with the salvaged hard drives. He dropped them on the floor, keeping his face perfectly neutral, though his heart was still racing from the near miss at the Annex. 

"We got the primary data cores out," Nick said smoothly. "The ghost-loop is burned. ZIA has nothing but melted plastic."

"They shouldn't have been there at all!" Ragnar snarled, taking a heavy, threatening step toward Nick. "That rabbit. Hopps. She was at the sub-station. She was at the decoy. And today she brought the feds right to our front door. She is a liability."

"She's a beat cop who got lucky," Skye said, stepping between Ragnar and Nick. Her voice was as cold and hard as the concrete floor. 

"Luck runs out," Ragnar growled, his eyes narrowing at the white fox. "I'm going to find her. I'm going to mount her head on the bumper of my transport, and I'm going to drive it past Precinct One. I'll show this city what happens when they interfere with my operations."

Nick’s claws dug into the palms of his paws so hard they almost broke the skin. He forced himself to remain completely still. If he defended Judy now, Ragnar would kill them both. 

"You're not touching her," Skye commanded, her tone slicing through Ragnar's bluster like a whip. "The Architect was very clear. No high-profile assassinations of law enforcement personnel without his direct authorization. If you kill a ZPD hero in broad daylight, you don't just bring the ZIA down on us. You bring the military. You jeopardize the entire timeline."

Ragnar loomed over her, his massive frame casting a long shadow over the white fox. "She knows too much."

"She knows nothing," Skye countered, not backing down an inch. "She thinks we're stealing hardware. Let her think it. We accelerate the final phase. In three days, it won't matter what she knows. The grid will belong to us."

Ragnar stared at her for a long, tense moment. The air in the room felt thick enough to choke on. Finally, the reptile let out a low, rumbling hiss. He didn't agree. He didn't say fine. He just turned his massive back to them and walked away toward the armory, his tail lashing angrily across the floor. 

Skye gave Nick a fractional nod. They picked up the duffel bags and walked toward their quarters. Nick kept his eyes forward, his mind racing. They had bought Judy some time. It was a fragile victory, but it was something. 

The heavy door to their quarters clicked shut. 

In the main hall, Ragnar waited until the sound of their footsteps faded completely. He turned back to the ruined planning table, his jaw flexing. 

Vesper walked over, delicately stepping around the shattered electronics. She picked up a surviving tablet and wiped the screen with her sleeve. 

"Skye is right, you know," Vesper said softly, her golden eyes fixed on the screen. "A loud, messy execution would be catastrophic for our optics. The Architect needs the city to view him as a savior, not a butcher."

"I don't care about his optics," Ragnar growled, leaning his heavy hands on the table. "The rabbit is a bloodhound. She tracked the ghost-loop. She tracked the shell companies. If she keeps digging, she's going to find the final node before we can lock it down."

"I agree," Vesper said simply. 

Ragnar looked at her, surprised. "You do?"

"Of course." Vesper finally looked up, a slow, chilling smile spreading across her muzzle. "Skye is thinking like a soldier. She sees rules and parameters. But we are not soldiers, Ragnar. We are problem solvers."

"You just said we can't kill her."

"I said we can't be *seen* killing her," Vesper corrected him, her voice dropping to a silken purr. "There is a difference between a public execution and a tragic, unfortunate accident in the line of duty. The streets of Zootopia are so dangerous these days. Anyone could catch a stray bullet. Or suffer a sudden... medical event."

Ragnar’s eyes narrowed. "Can you guarantee it won't trace back to us?"

"My dear Ragnar," Vesper said, reaching into her coat and pulling out a small, steel case. She opened it, revealing a row of glass vials filled with clear liquid. "I don't leave traces. I leave mysteries."

Ragnar looked at the vials, then back at the caracal. A slow, brutal grin mirrored her own. 

"Do it," Ragnar said. "I want her dead before tomorrow morning."
