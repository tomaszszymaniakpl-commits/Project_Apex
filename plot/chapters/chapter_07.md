# Chapter 07: Dead Men Tell No Lies

*ZNN BREAKING NEWS: TRAGEDY IN THE HARBOR DISTRICT. ZPD HERO AMONG CASUALTIES.*

The voice of the snow leopard anchor was professionally somber, echoing softly from the concealed surround-sound speakers built into the ceiling.

*"...emergency services battled the chemical fire for six hours before bringing it under control. While the ZPD has yet to release an official statement regarding the exact cause of the blast, preliminary reports suggest a tragic accident during a routine pursuit. Authorities have confirmed that Detective Nicholas Wilde, widely celebrated for his role in the Night Howler case, was killed in the explosion, alongside an unidentified second victim..."*

From the eighty-fifth floor of the highest glass tower in Sahara Square, the city of Zootopia didn't look like a place of mourning. It looked like a circuit board. 

The morning sun hit the downtown district, fracturing into brilliant, blinding spears of light. Below, the traffic on the main arteries was a slow-moving river of metallic red, white, and silver. Millions of mammals moving in their designated lanes, obeying their designated signals, completely unaware of the fragile systems holding their lives together.

A large, elegant paw reached out and pressed a button on a sleek remote. The news broadcast cut off instantly, leaving only the near-silent hum of the climate control system.

"A routine pursuit," the jaguar murmured to the empty room. A quiet, unreadable smile touched the corners of his mouth. "The system always protects its own image first. It builds a comforting narrative so the cogs keep turning. They would rather accept a tragedy than admit a fundamental flaw in their design."

He turned away from the massive flatscreen. The office was a masterpiece of minimalist architecture. Polished obsidian floors, stark white walls free of the usual corporate clutter, and a massive desk carved from a single piece of reclaimed ironwood. 

He wore a tailored charcoal suit that moved fluidly over his lean frame. The silver dusting the edges of his black rosettes caught the morning light as he slowly swirled the ice in his crystal tumbler. He took a sip of the infused water, his amber eyes drifting back to the panoramic view. The city was sprawling, chaotic, organic. It was begging for order.

The soft chime of the intercom broke the silence.

"Mr. Volt?" 

The jaguar pressed a clawed digit to the speaker on his desk. "Yes, Sarah."

"I have the Mayor's office on line two," the secretary’s voice crackled through, crisp and efficient. "They're asking if you're still available for the infrastructure ribbon-cutting ceremony this afternoon, given the... mood in the city today."

His smile returned, perfectly polite, perfectly engineered. 

"Please inform the Mayor's office that Volt Industries remains fully committed to Zootopia's future," he said, his voice a rich, resonant baritone. "We must push forward, now more than ever. I will be there."

"Right away, sir. Oh, and your ten o'clock design review has been pushed to eleven."

"Thank you, Sarah. Hold my calls until then."

He released the button. The office returned to its pristine silence. 

For a long moment, he simply stood there, watching the city bake in the morning heat. To anyone else, the burning of the harbor warehouse was a disaster. To him, it was merely an observation point—a small, necessary friction in a much larger equation.

His private cellular phone, resting on the corner of the ironwood desk, vibrated once. A short, sharp buzz.

He picked it up. He didn't check the caller ID. 

"Status," he said simply.

The voice on the other end was rough, gravelly, and entirely unsentimental. 

*"The package is secured."*

The jaguar took another slow sip of his water. The ice clinked gently against the crystal. 

"Very well," he replied. 

He ended the call and slipped the phone into his jacket pocket. He looked back out the window, his gaze drifting past the skyscrapers, toward the distant, climate-controlled walls of Tundratown. 

The city was so beautiful from up here. It was almost a shame the city had to break just enough to beg for a savior.

---

The air in the subterranean storm drain tasted of rust, stagnant water, and the sharp, metallic tang of unspent adrenaline. 

It was dark, lit only by the pale, stuttering glow of a single emergency bulb bolted to the concrete ceiling. The distant, muffled roar of the chemical fire above ground reverberated through the pipes like the heartbeat of a dying giant. 

Nick Wilde sat slumped against the curved, wet wall of the drain. 

His ZPD tactical vest was gone, lost somewhere in the black water of the canal they had used to escape. His shirt was a ruined, scorched mess. The fur on his right arm was burned down to the skin, and a deep laceration ran across his shoulder. 

He didn't feel it. He didn't feel anything except the crushing, suffocating weight inside his chest.

The white vixen was kneeling beside him, her pristine coat stained with soot and harbor mud. She held a small, military-grade medical kit, her paws moving with ruthless efficiency. She poured a generous amount of clear antiseptic directly into the long cut on his shoulder.

The liquid bubbled violently. Nick didn't even flinch. His emerald eyes were locked onto the opposite wall, staring through the concrete, through the earth, seeing only a ghost in the rain. 

He watched the vixen’s paws expertly thread a surgical needle. Those paws. Slender, white, stained with grime.

He remembered the first time he had seen them after months of silence. 

*Three nights earlier.*

*The rain had been falling in sheets in the Rainforest District. Nick stood under the awning of an abandoned safehouse, his heart hammering a frantic rhythm against his ribs. The text message on his phone burned in his mind. The code they had sworn only to use if there was no way out. Code Blue.*

*A figure approached through the downpour, wearing a heavy, dark raincoat with the hood pulled low. They stepped under the awning. The figure reached up with white paws and pulled the hood back.*

*Nick's breath hitched. The world seemed to stop spinning.*

*"Code Blue," he rasped, his voice cracking instantly. "You said you'd only use it if you were dead. You said you were on the Outback Islands."*

*She didn't answer. She stepped forward and grabbed his face with both paws, her thumbs pressing into his cheeks, pulling his forehead down until it touched hers.*

*"Skye," Nick breathed, his voice breaking completely. "Is it really you?"*

*He pulled her into a desperate, crushing embrace. He buried his face in her shoulder, the terror of the last six months—the disconnected numbers, the unanswered letters, the silent, gnawing fear that he had lost the only family he had left—momentarily washed away. She was alive. She was here.*

*But the relief only lasted a second. Nick pulled back, his paws gripping her shoulders. The fear instantly mutated into fury.*

*"Why did you stop answering?!" he demanded, shaking her slightly. "I thought something happened to you! Mom hasn't slept properly in months! You lied to me, Skye! You never left the city, did you?"*

*Skye looked up at him, her blue eyes bright with unshed tears, but her jaw was set. "I couldn't leave, Nick. Not yet."*

*"We agreed to let it go," Nick hissed, his ears pinning back. "When I met Judy, when I got the badge... we agreed. We were going to stop chasing ghosts and live our lives."*

*"I wanted you to have your life!" Skye shot back, her voice a fierce whisper. "You found a partner. You found a reason to stay in the light. If I told you I was staying in the dark, you would have stayed with me. I lied so you could be free."*

*"Then why are you here now?"*

*"Because I found them," she said. The words hit Nick like a physical blow. "The people who ordered the hit on Dad. It's bigger than we thought. Bigger than street crews. I've been inside for almost two years, Nick. And they are planning something that will tear this city down to the bedrock."*

*Nick stared at his little sister. The sweet kit who used to draw pictures of police officers at the kitchen table was gone. In her place was a hardened, exhausted operative balancing on the edge of a knife.*

*"I'm so close to the top," Skye continued, her voice urgent but steady. "But they are preparing for their endgame. They need municipal cryptographic keys. Things buried deep in the ZPD vaults. They need a dirty cop to get them, and they need that cop to help them navigate the city's blind spots afterward."*

*Nick felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He saw where this was going.*

*"I gave them your name, Nick."*

*The rain hammered against the tin roof above them. Nick stepped back, shaking his head slowly, disbelief warring with anger.*

*"You sold me out?" he whispered.*

*"I sold them a ghost!" Skye shot back, grabbing his jacket again. "I sold them the hustler you used to be! I told them you were disillusioned, underpaid, and ready to turn. They bought it. If you do this—if you steal those keys and hand them over—they will bring you inside. We'll be together. We'll be at the heart of their operation."*

*Nick pulled away from her, running a paw over his wet face. "Are you insane? You're asking me to rob my own Chief of Police. To burn my badge. To become everything I spent the last three years proving I wasn't."*

*"I'm asking you to help me finish what Dad started," Skye said, her blue eyes blazing in the dark. "But this isn't just about revenge anymore. They are preparing something massive, Nick. Something that will tear this city down to the bedrock. And we can't go to the authorities."*

*"Why not?" Nick challenged, though his gut already knew the answer.*

*"Because they own them," she whispered bitterly. "They have friends in places we can't touch. If we go official, the evidence disappears—and then we disappear. We have to go rogue. We get inside, we find the architect of this whole thing, and we cut the head off the snake. But I can't do it alone anymore. I am drowning in there, Nick."*

*Nick looked away, staring into the dark, rain-swept street. He thought of the quiet mornings. He thought of the pride in his mother's eyes when he graduated the academy. And he thought of Judy.*

*"Do you know what you're asking me to do?" Nick asked, his voice dropping to a harsh, ragged whisper. "If I do this... Judy will hunt me. She won't stop. And it will break her."*

*Skye’s expression softened with genuine sorrow, but her resolve didn't waver. "She has to believe it, Nick. The people we are dealing with... if they even suspect she's involved, or that this is a cover, they won't just kill us. They'll kill her. You have to lie to her. It's the only way to protect her."*

*Nick closed his eyes. The weight of the world pressed down on his shoulders, suffocating and absolute. He was choosing between his sister's life, his father's ghost, and his own future.*

*"I'll get the keys," Nick said finally, opening his eyes. They were dead and flat. "We do this fast. We get inside, we burn them down, and we get out."*

*The sting of the surgical needle pulling through his flesh snapped Nick back to the present.*

He was in the storm drain. The clean infiltration was gone. His life was gone.

Nick suddenly surged forward with a guttural roar. He violently shoved Skye's hands away, ignoring the tearing of his own skin. He scrambled to his feet, his claws scraping against the wet concrete, his chest heaving.

"Fuck!" Nick screamed. The word echoed down the tunnel, raw and jagged. He picked up a rusted piece of rebar from the floor and hurled it as hard as he could into the darkness. It shattered against the wall with a deafening *CLANG*. 

"Nick, sit down, you're tearing the stitches—" Skye started, standing up with both paws raised.

"The whole plan is fucked, Skye! She was supposed to stay out of this!" Nick spun on her, his fangs bared, his eyes wild and luminous in the dim light. "How the hell did she even find us?! It was supposed to be a clean handoff! I give you the case, I walk away a traitor, we rendezvous later! Why the hell did you blow the warehouse?!"

"I didn't know the chemicals were unstable!" Skye shouted back, not backing down an inch. She matched his volume, her own stress bleeding through. "I was throwing a smoke charge to give us cover!"

"We didn't need cover! I was handling it!"

"No, you weren't!" Skye stepped into his space, pointing a hard finger at his chest. "Your partner ruined the drop! The moment she stepped out from behind those crates, the entire plan died! They had a sniper watching us from the roof, Nick! She walked right into the crosshairs! If I hadn't thrown that charge to blind him, we would all be dead right now!"

Nick stopped. The fury drained out of him, replaced instantly by a freezing, absolute horror. 

He remembered the docks. He remembered Judy standing in the rain, begging him for an explanation, begging him to lie to her. 

And then, he remembered the red dot. 

It had appeared from the darkness above the warehouse roof. A tiny, lethal laser sight, settling perfectly over Judy's heart. Skye had seen it first. Nick had seen it a second later. 

If he had dropped the case, the sniper would have fired. If he had warned her, the sniper would have fired. He had three seconds to convince the invisible killer that he wasn't a cop anymore, and he had to convince Judy to back away.

*"Predators don't change, Carrots. It was always about the money."*

He had looked into the eyes of the only mammal who ever truly believed in him, and destroyed her soul, just to make sure her heart kept beating.
In the memory, her lavender eyes went wide—not with fear, but with something worse: betrayal.

Nick staggered backward until his spine hit the curved wall of the drain. He slid down, burying his face in his blood-stained paws. His shoulders began to shake. The great, cynical Nick Wilde, the fox who always had an answer, was crying in the dark.

"She was so close to the fire," Nick choked out, the words muffled by his paws. "Did she make it? Tell me she made it."

Skye's expression softened. The hardened operative vanished, leaving only a sister watching her brother break. She reached into her pocket and pulled out a heavy, encrypted comms device. She stared at the small screen for a long moment.

A single message glowed in the green text.

*Rabbit is alive.*

"She's alive," Skye said quietly, kneeling beside him. "She made it."

Nick let out a shuddering breath, his head falling back against the concrete. "I destroyed her, Skye. I destroyed everything."

"You saved her life," Skye corrected firmly. She placed a gentle paw on his uninjured shoulder. "Listen to me. You are a ghost now. The city thinks you're dead. They think you're a traitor who proved his loyalty. We don't have a way out anymore, Nick. We can only go deeper."

Nick stared into the pitch-black tunnel ahead of them. The heavy steel door at the far end of the storm drain shrieked on rusted hinges, tearing through the quiet intimacy of the moment. 

Nick didn’t hesitate. The exhaustion, the grief, the tears—he buried them all in a fraction of a second behind a reinforced titanium wall in his mind. The mask slammed back into place so hard it almost physically hurt. By the time the heavy footsteps reached the edge of the emergency light's halo, Nick was sitting up, his expression molded into a mask of arrogant boredom, his emerald eyes flattening into polished glass.

Two figures stepped out of the shadows.

The first was a caracal in sleek black tactical gear. Vesper Nyx. She moved with a frightening, silent grace, carrying a heavy, waterproof case that undoubtedly held the municipal keys Nick had stolen.

The second figure didn't walk; he stalked. 

Ragnar Drakov was an absolute nightmare of scale and muscle. The Komodo dragon was nearly as broad as a rhino, covered in thick, heavily scarred, moss-green scales. He wore a dark, heavy combat harness, and the air around him smelled faintly of sulfur and dried blood. 

He didn't say a word. He crossed the distance between the door and Nick in three massive strides.

Before Skye could even stand up, Ragnar's massive, clawed hand shot out. He grabbed Nick by the throat, lifting the fox entirely off the wet concrete. 

Nick choked, his claws instinctively scrabbling against the thick scales of the dragon’s forearm. The grip was like an industrial vice. 

"You got sloppy, fox," Ragnar hissed. His voice was a wet, guttural rumble that vibrated in Nick’s chest. A long, forked tongue flicked out, tasting the air inches from Nick’s face. "The rabbit shouldn't have been there. She saw the handoff. She forced the blast. We lost a staging ground and a quarter of our chemical reserves because you couldn't control your old pet."

Skye took a step forward, her hand dropping to the tactical knife strapped to her thigh. 

"Ragnar, put him down," she said, her voice dropping an octave, slipping seamlessly into her own criminal persona. "He got the keys. He did his job."

"He compromised the operation," Ragnar snarled, squeezing tighter. Nick’s vision began to swim with black spots, his legs kicking uselessly above the floor. "I should have put a bullet in the rabbit's skull from the roof when I had the chance. And I should snap your neck right now to tie up the loose ends."

Nick’s lungs were burning. Panic—pure, animal panic—screamed at him to fight back, to thrash, to beg. 

He did none of those things. He went completely limp.

He forced his lips to curl into a jagged, bloodstained smirk. He looked down his nose at the massive reptile, meeting the cold, reptilian eyes with absolute, mocking defiance.

"You're... welcome," Nick wheezed, forcing the words past the crushing pressure on his windpipe. 

Ragnar’s eyes narrowed. "What did you say?"

"I said... you're welcome," Nick gasped out, a dark chuckle rattling in his chest. "I walked into the most secure precinct in the city. I lifted the keys right out of the Chief's pocket while he was looking at me. I delivered the entire city's infrastructure... wrapped in a bow. And you're crying about some... spilled chemicals?"

Nick forced his grin wider, exposing his fangs, playing the arrogant hustler to the hilt. "If you wanted a clean, quiet job... you should have hired a librarian, Lizzy. You hired me. I got it done. If you want to kill me for it, go ahead. See how well your boss takes it when you bring him a corpse instead of an asset."

Ragnar’s jaw tightened. The muscles in his massive arm twitched. For a second, Nick thought he had miscalculated. He thought the dragon was just going to crush his trachea and be done with it.

"Put him down, Ragnar." 

Vesper’s voice cut through the tension. It wasn't a yell; it was a cold, absolute command. She was standing a few feet away, watching the spectacle with the detached interest of a scientist observing rats in a maze.

"He's an arrogant liability," Ragnar growled, not loosening his grip.

"He is the Architect's property," Vesper corrected smoothly. Her amber eyes shifted to Nick. "The Architect requires someone with an intimate knowledge of the ZPD's blind spots. The fox has proven he can deliver. He stays."

Ragnar held Nick for one agonizing second longer. Then, with a disgusted snort, he opened his hand.

Nick hit the concrete hard, collapsing onto his paws and knees. He gasped for air, coughing violently as oxygen rushed back into his starved lungs. He rubbed his bruised throat, keeping his head down to hide the sheer terror in his eyes.

"You're in our world now, Wilde," Ragnar spat, turning away. "You step out of line again, and I won't wait for permission. And the next time I see your little rabbit friend... I won't just use a laser pointer."

The dragon stalked back down the tunnel, disappearing into the shadows without another word.

Nick stayed on his hands and knees, fighting to get his breathing under control. Vesper walked past him, her silent footsteps carrying her toward the exit. She stopped, looking down at the ruined, gasping fox.

She reached into a pouch on her tactical vest and tossed something onto the wet concrete in front of him. A clean, white towel.

"Welcome to the new world, Wilde," Vesper said, her voice entirely devoid of emotion. "I advise you not to make any more friends. It seems to be bad for your health."

She turned and followed Ragnar into the dark.

Nick slowly picked up the towel. He pressed it to his bleeding shoulder, closing his eyes. The mask was secure. The performance was flawless, and for the first time since he was a kit, silence felt heavier than fear.
